History of Pakistan

Pakistan is a country shaped by ancient civilizations, the movement for a separate homeland, and the many languages and cultures of its people. Its story reaches from the Indus Valley to a modern nation at the crossroads of South and Central Asia.

Explore the timeline ↓

A brief timeline

The land that is now Pakistan has been home to settled communities for thousands of years. These milestones offer a starting point, not the whole story.

c. 2600–1900 BCE

Indus Valley Civilization

Major urban centers such as Mohenjo-daro and Harappa flourished along the Indus and its tributaries, with planned streets, craft production, and long-distance trade.

711 CE onward

Many cultures meet

Over centuries, the region became home to diverse peoples, languages, and traditions. Muslim dynasties and later the Mughal Empire left a lasting mark on its history and architecture.

1858–1947

British rule and the independence movement

After the end of East India Company rule, the region was governed by the British Raj. Political movements grew, including the demand for a homeland for Muslims of the subcontinent.

14 August 1947

Pakistan is founded

British India was partitioned and Pakistan became an independent state. The creation of the country was accompanied by mass migration and immense human hardship.

1956–1973

A republic and a new constitution

Pakistan adopted its first constitution in 1956. Following the events of 1971 and the emergence of Bangladesh, the 1973 Constitution established the framework of the present republic.

1998–today

Pakistan in the modern era

Pakistan conducted nuclear tests in 1998. Today it is a federal parliamentary republic whose society, economy, and international relationships continue to evolve.

Pakistan · Conflict and history

Wars and armed conflicts

An overview of major wars involving Pakistan, alongside significant border clashes and internal armed conflicts. These events had different causes and outcomes, and brought profound loss and displacement to civilians.

Wars and conflicts

Entries are in chronological order. Some were declared wars between states; others were limited military confrontations or internal insurgencies. This overview covers major conflicts, not every operation or skirmish.

1947–1949

First Kashmir War

Fighting between India and Pakistan began in 1947 over the princely state of Jammu and Kashmir, whose accession was disputed. A UN-backed ceasefire took effect on 1 January 1949, leaving the territory divided and its political status unresolved.

1965

Second India–Pakistan War

Major fighting took place across the international border and in Kashmir. A UN-brokered ceasefire ended the war, and the two governments later signed the Tashkent Declaration in January 1966.

1971

India–Pakistan War and the creation of Bangladesh

After a severe political crisis and conflict in East Pakistan, war between India and Pakistan ended with the surrender of Pakistani forces in Dhaka. Bangladesh became independent. The period involved immense human suffering and displacement.

1984–present

Siachen conflict

India and Pakistan deployed forces to the Siachen Glacier in a dispute over the boundary's interpretation. A 2003 ceasefire greatly reduced firing, but troops have remained deployed in the high-altitude region, where severe weather has caused many deaths.

1999

Kargil conflict

Indian and Pakistani forces fought in the Kargil district near the Line of Control in Kashmir. The conflict ended after Pakistan withdrew its forces amid international diplomatic pressure.

1948–present, episodic

Balochistan insurgencies

Several waves of armed rebellion have taken place in Balochistan, including episodes beginning in 1948, 1958, 1963, 1973, and the early 2000s. The conflicts involve separatist groups and the Pakistani state, with political, economic, and security dimensions.

2004–present

Militancy and counterinsurgency in the northwest

Militant groups and Pakistani security forces have fought across parts of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and the former tribal districts. Attacks and military operations have caused significant civilian casualties and displacement. This is an internal armed conflict, not a war between countries.

January 2024

Iran–Pakistan cross-border strikes

Iran and Pakistan exchanged strikes across their border, with each government saying it targeted militant groups. Both countries condemned the other's actions as violations of sovereignty, then moved to restore diplomatic relations. This was a brief military exchange, not a declared war.

May 2025

India–Pakistan military conflict

Following a deadly attack in Kashmir, India and Pakistan exchanged missile, drone, and artillery strikes over several days. A ceasefire was announced on 10 May. The countries reported casualties and disputed claims about damage and military losses.

Pakistan · Constitution and legislation

Pakistan's laws, 1947–2026

Pakistan's legal system has changed through new constitutions, constitutional amendments, parliamentary Acts, and provincial legislation. This guide explains how those changes are made and highlights major milestones through October 2026.

How laws are made

The Constitution sets the rules for legislation. Federal and provincial laws follow different routes, and constitutional amendments require a higher threshold than ordinary Acts.

Federal bill

Proposal, debate, and votes

A bill may be introduced by the government or a member of Parliament. It is debated, may be examined by a committee, and is voted on. Most federal bills must pass both the National Assembly and Senate; money bills have a special procedure and begin in the National Assembly.

Assent and publication

A bill becomes an Act

After Parliament passes a bill, it is sent to the President for assent under the Constitution. Once enacted and published, it becomes federal law. Provincial assemblies make laws within their constitutional powers, with the required provincial assent.

Constitutional change

A higher voting threshold

Under Article 239, a constitutional amendment must be passed by at least two-thirds of the total membership of each House of Parliament and receive presidential assent. An amendment changing a province's boundaries also needs approval by at least two-thirds of that province's assembly.

When Parliament is not sitting

Temporary ordinances

The Constitution permits the President to promulgate an ordinance when Parliament is not in session and immediate action is necessary. Ordinances are temporary and must be placed before Parliament; they are not a substitute for a permanent Act.

Legal milestones

A selected constitutional timeline, not a catalogue of every federal and provincial statute. Constitutional amendments change the Constitution; ordinary Acts create or update laws under it.

1947

Independence and interim legal framework

The Indian Independence Act created the new dominions. Pakistan initially adapted the Government of India Act 1935 as its interim constitutional framework, while many existing laws continued until replaced or amended.

1949

Objectives Resolution

Pakistan's Constituent Assembly adopted the Objectives Resolution, setting out principles to guide constitution-making. It later became a substantive constitutional provision through the Eighth Amendment in 1985.

1956

First Constitution

The Constituent Assembly enacted Pakistan's first constitution, which took effect on 23 March. It made Pakistan a republic with a parliamentary system. The constitution was abrogated after the 1958 military coup.

1962–1970

Presidential constitution and transition

A new constitution introduced a presidential system in 1962. It was suspended in 1969. The 1970 Legal Framework Order then set rules for elections and the proposed constitution-making process.

1973

The current Constitution

Elected representatives negotiated and approved the Constitution in Parliament on 10 April; it came into force on 14 August. It established a federal parliamentary system, fundamental rights, and a procedure for future amendments.

1974

Second Amendment

Parliament passed the Second Amendment, changing the Constitution's definition of who is Muslim and legally classifying Ahmadis as non-Muslims. It remains a significant and contested change in Pakistan's constitutional history.

1985

Eighth Amendment

The amendment restored the Constitution after military rule and validated a range of earlier actions. It also strengthened presidential powers, including the power to dissolve the National Assembly.

1997–2003

Presidential powers shift again

The Thirteenth Amendment in 1997 removed the President's power to dismiss the National Assembly. The Seventeenth Amendment in 2003 restored that power in modified form after another period of military rule.

2010

Eighteenth Amendment

A broad parliamentary amendment restored a stronger parliamentary system, transferred many subjects to the provinces, and renamed the North-West Frontier Province as Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.

2018

Twenty-fifth Amendment

Parliament and the provincial assembly approved the merger of the former Federally Administered Tribal Areas with Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, changing constitutional and administrative arrangements for the region.

2024

Twenty-sixth Amendment

Parliament enacted changes to judicial appointments and constitutional case procedures, including a new process for selecting the Chief Justice of Pakistan and constitutional benches.

2025

Twenty-seventh Amendment

Parliament passed the amendment on 13 November. It established a Federal Constitutional Court and changed provisions on judicial structure and military command, including Article 243.

2026

New Acts under the amended Constitution

Parliament continued passing ordinary legislation. Examples listed by the National Assembly include the Finance Act 2026 and the Defense Forces of Pakistan Act 2026. These Acts are statutes, not a new constitution or constitutional amendment; the official Acts index is current through 1 October 2026.
